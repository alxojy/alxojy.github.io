Doing hard things sucks. A lot.

I don't think people say that enough. Whenever I'm in the middle of something hard, a big part of me just wants to quit. And most of the time, quitting honestly feels like the sensible thing to do.

Recently I spent way too long on one dyno at the gym. If you don't climb, a dyno is basically a jump where you let go of the wall and hope you catch the next hold. I didn't catch it. Like, 20 times. Every time I came off I'd sit on the mat, stare at the wall, and tell myself "ok, last one". It was never the last one.

It got me thinking about why I keep doing this to myself.

When I was a kid, my (Asian) parents signed me up for Kumon. I had arithmetic math drilled into my head, every single day. Suffice to say I did not enjoy it at all. I did them because I had to, not because I wanted to.

The funny thing is, it worked. I'm really good at math now. I can do multiplications in my head faster than most people can open the calculator app, which is weirdly useful at the grocery store lol. I never would have signed up for it myself, but I'm kind of glad I did.

By the time I was older though, nobody had to make me study anymore. No one was pushing me except me. I wanted the grades, so I put in the hours, even when it was boring and I really didn't feel like it. Honestly, I was extremely miserable a lot of the time. But I was chasing that high of placing first in my grade. That feeling made all the misery worth it, at least for a while. And it paid off. My good grades led to a scholarship.

I read up a bit on this and apparently there's a name for it: grit. I came across this TED talk and it kind of put words to what I'd been feeling.

{{< youtube H14bBuluwB8 >}}

TLDR; the people who make it aren't always the smartest or the most talented. They're just the ones who don't give up.

Looking back, it's kind of funny. As a kid, I had to be forced to do hard things. In school, I forced myself. And now I literally pay to fall off plastic rocks for fun. I'm not saying I love all hard things now. Life still throws plenty of hard stuff at me that I'd happily avoid. But the hard things I get to pick myself, where I'm the one in control and it's up to me whether I keep going? Those I've actually started to enjoy.

It's not that hard things got any less sucky. They still suck. Attempt 25 on a dyno you can't land really eats your ego. But I think that's kind of the point. When I pick the hard thing myself, I'm basically practising being uncomfortable on my own terms. And every time I get through it, I come out a bit more resilient, and a bit better at not falling apart. So when life throws the hard stuff I didn't sign up for, I've at least had some practice.

So I guess that's why we do hard things. Not because they're fun (they're not), but because they get us ready for the ones we don't get to choose.
